challenges1.mp3: The road was not easy. For every problem, we changed one thing only, then tested again. These are the challenges of building Masmak, and how we solved them. We forgot the Raspberry Pi password: we booted it in repair mode from the SD card and set a new one, without losing a single file. The camera did not work on the Raspberry Pi: so we moved eye tracking to the laptop camera, which sends commands over Wi-Fi. The pupil indices were reversed: we printed the raw numbers, moved our eyes, and fixed the order. A lost message made Masmak over-rotate: we added a watchdog that stops it after one second with no message. Videos were shorter than their audio: we repeated the last frame until the audio ended, and kept the originals. The audio was far too quiet: we raised it in three places: the sound card, the system, and the player. The camera dropped when the laptop slept: we blocked sleep, and added a guard that restarts it by itself. The board and the Pi swapped addresses, so commands got lost: we gave every device a fixed address.
challenges2.mp3: Then the safety robots and add-ons brought new challenges. The flame sensor was actually a heat sensor: we tested it with a candle and replaced it with a real flame sensor. A board burned, because a battery labelled five volts was really seven point four: now we measure with a multimeter, use a five-volt converter, and power the board from a power bank. The wheels ran by themselves at full speed: we added a separate ground wire. The Leader robot missed the black line: we turned off Wi-Fi power saving. The robots could not find the iPhone hotspot: we turned on maximize compatibility, and opened the hotspot page before starting. The wheelchair worked only by cable: we installed a system that works over Bluetooth, and it stops by itself if the link drops.
