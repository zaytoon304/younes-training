cover.mp3: Honorable judges, welcome. We are proud to present our project: Smart Al Masmak… a heritage museum that welcomes everyone, and protects everyone.
problem.mp3: Heritage belongs to all of us… but not everyone can reach it. Visitors with motor disabilities struggle to move around fixed models. Visitors with hearing disabilities miss the guide's explanation. Visitors with visual disabilities cannot see the details on display. And heritage built of mud and wood burns fast, while crowds need time to get out.
solution.mp3: So we built one smart museum that protects people and heritage together. First: explore. The visitor turns the palace with his eyes, with sign language and audio. Second: protect people. A smart gate knows who is inside, and a team of robots fights fire. Third: protect the artifact. Sensors watch the air around the model, day and night.
explore.mp3: We start with exploring: heritage you control with your eyes only. The visitor looks right or left, and the palace turns slowly on its turntable. He holds his gaze, the palace stops, and that side tells its story. With sign language for deaf visitors, and audio narration for blind visitors. And a wheelchair we built from LEGO bricks brings the visitor to the station.
gate.mp3: Because people come first, we added a smart entrance gate. Every visitor receives a wristband with his name. He taps it on the reader at the entrance, the barrier opens, with a green light and a welcome sound, and the museum always knows how many people are inside, and who they are. If there is a fire, the gate opens by itself, and the names of everyone inside are sent to Civil Defense.
artifact.mp3: Protecting the artifact itself matters just as much. Inside the Masmak case, sensors take care of it. A temperature sensor, because heat damages mud and wood. A humidity sensor, because moisture causes cracks and stains. And an air quality sensor, for dust and pollution. A screen shows the readings live, and if the air becomes dangerous for the artifact, the system warns and acts automatically.
robots.mp3: Fire is handled by a team of robots that decide by themselves. The sensor station detects flame and gas, and warns with a screen and an alarm. The Leader robot decides: fire, it goes itself. Gas, it sends the gas robot. Both at once, both robots move together. Each robot stops at the black line, sprays water or fans the gas away, then returns home. And the Leader knows its limit: if the fire is still burning after fifteen seconds, it calls for help.
civil.mp3: This is where people come in: Civil Defense. A Telegram alert arrives at once: fire on the east side, and how many people are inside. Then a request for support, with a safe passage to enter. A firefighter drives two robots with hand gestures, watching the fire through a live camera, from a safe place. And a rescue robot guides the visitors to the exit. When the fire is out, the last message arrives: Masmak Museum is safe.
testing.mp3: We tested every part alone, then the whole system together. Eye control on all four sides: passed. Fire only: the Leader sprays and returns. Passed. Gas only: the gas robot fans it away and returns. Passed. Fire and gas together: both robots at once. Passed. And a fire that does not go out: the hand-driven support robots. Passed.
impact.mp3: Masmak is only our model… any museum in the world can use our system. An innovative idea from Saudi Arabia, the land of innovation.
live.mp3: And now… we present the project to you, live.
